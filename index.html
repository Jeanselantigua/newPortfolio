Case study

Highbridge Voices

A Bronx youth choir needed a public website that could take applications and donations, and a private portal so staff and students were not running the year out of inboxes and spreadsheets. I was the only developer. Both are in production at highbridgevoices.org.

Problem

The public site was a WordPress leftover that was hard to update. Concert pages, program applications, and donation flows kept breaking or lagging behind the season. Inside the organization, rehearsal music lived in messy unsorted cabinets and spreadsheets that kept dissapearing, the calendar was wherever someone last emailed it, and attendance and roster work happened offsite.

Solution

I rebuilt the public site programs, apply, donate, benefit concert, news and built a staff/student portal on React, Node, Express, and PostgreSQL. JWT login with roles. Announcements, calendar, choir tracks (unique IDs, A–Z, filter by voice part), roster, attendance, applications, and a music/audio library. Deployed on Vercel and Railway. I still ship the seasonal pages.

Result

Staff and students use the portal during the school year. The public site handles enrollment and fundraising. When the benefit concert needed tickets, ads, and an electronic journal, those went onto the same site instead of a one off tool.

Process

How the work actually goes

Same sequence for every job — a personal site, an organization site, or something custom. If a step is missing, the site looks finished and then nobody can run it.

  1. Map how you work today

    Who visits, who updates the site, what people need to find, fill out, or buy. I write that down before I open a design file. A personal site, a clinic, and a membership tool all start here — the answers are just different.

  2. Draw the pages

    Sitemap first. If the job has bookings, a shop, accounts, or a dashboard, the flows and fields come next so we are not inventing them in week six. You see the structure and say what is wrong while it is still simple to change.

  3. Build it and put it in front of you

    I design and develop the thing myself. You get a staging URL and click through before we go live and before any old site comes down, if there is one.

  4. Stay after launch

    Sites do not freeze on launch day. Events, products, forms, and new pages show up later. I keep a maintenance relationship so those land on the same site instead of a new vendor each time.
